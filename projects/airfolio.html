Independent product design · Provisional patent filed

Airfolio

One part · TPU · Patent pending

Two things you already own, made into the one thing Apple stopped making.

The problem

I wanted music at the gym and on runs without carrying a phone. An Apple Watch and a pair of AirPods can already do that — the watch stores the music and drives the buds, no phone in the loop. But they're two loose objects plus a charging case, which is three things to lose in a gym bag, and a watch is an awkward thing to operate while you're moving.

What I actually wanted already existed once: an iPod. One object, in one hand, with the earbuds living on it.

The part

Airfolio is a printed TPU body in the shape of the thing it's replacing. An Apple Watch drops into the top, so its screen becomes the display and the controls. A pair of AirPods sits in the base. Together they're one device you hold, with a real face and real buttons, and no phone anywhere near it.

  • One part. No fasteners, no assembly, no electronics, nothing extra to charge. The watch and the buds are unmodified and come straight back out.
  • TPU, on purpose. A flexible print lets the retention come from the material instead of a latch — the watch and the buds are held by the walls flexing around them.
  • Designed around two products I didn't design. Which meant measuring both, modeling to those dimensions, and holding tolerances tight enough to grip without marking anything.
  • Shaped to be held. The proportions are an iPod's on purpose. It reads as one object rather than an accessory with something clipped to it.

Process

Modeled in SolidWorks, printed in TPU, used, then revised — mostly around retention. A grip that holds on the bench doesn't necessarily hold at mile three, and a grip strong enough for mile three can be too tight to get the buds out with cold hands. Most of the design time went into that one trade-off.

Once the fit worked, I filed and received a provisional patent on the design.
