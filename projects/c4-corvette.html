The job GM buried behind the water pump, and everything else a thirty-five-year-old car asks for.

Optispark

The C4's Optispark distributor sits at the front of the engine, driven off the camshaft and sealed behind the water pump. When it fails you get misfires and hard starting, and the repair means coolant out, water pump off, and careful work to get the new unit indexed and sealed correctly. Reassemble it wrong and you're doing the whole job twice.

I diagnosed the fault, replaced the Optispark, and got the car running properly again. It's the project I'd point to for diagnosis rather than fabrication: reading the symptoms, ruling things out, and committing to the teardown only once the evidence supported it.

Restomod work

  • LED headlight conversion. Modern lighting in place of the original sealed units — a real safety difference on dark roads, and the C4's pop-up housings make the swap more interesting than a bulb change.
  • Badging. Updated exterior badges to clean up the presentation.
  • Body work. Basic panel and finish repair, ongoing.
  • Service. Oil, coolant, and the rest of the maintenance that decides whether a car this age is a project or a driver.

Why it's on here

Fabrication and CAD get the attention, but a car you can't diagnose is a car you can't modify. This one taught me to work on something with real consequences for getting it wrong, and to be patient with a repair that doesn't reward shortcuts.
