Graphite → soldered wire

Practice, and the fastest way I know to find out whether a shape actually works.

Drawing

Freehand, no CAD, but laid out properly: side, plan, and front views of the same car on one page, so they have to agree with each other. That's the useful discipline — a three-quarter sketch will happily hide a proportion you got wrong, and an orthographic set won't.

What I'm working on is proportion — wheel-to-body relationship, dash-to-axle, greenhouse height — and stance, because a car that sits wrong looks wrong no matter how good the surfacing is.

Wire model

Then I bent and soldered a wire model to those views and set it back down on the drawing it came from, which is the whole test: the model either agrees with the paper or it doesn't. A drawing only has to be right from one angle. A model has to be right from all of them, so the compromises you got away with on paper show up immediately — a shoulder line that doesn't resolve into the rear, a roof that reads well in profile and looks heavy from the front.

Bending and soldering the form also meant thinking about it as sections, which is closer to how a surface gets built in CAD than a sketch ever is.

Where it connects

This is the design half of the engineering. Fabrication taught me what a shape costs to make; sketching is where I work out whether it's worth making. I'd like to keep doing both.
